**The Fable of the Sun and the Moon**



**The Creation of the Sun and the Moon**



Before daylight existed, the Mesoamerican gods gathered at Teotihuacan to decide who would illuminate the world. Only two gods volunteered:Tecuciztecatl and Nanahuatzin. A penance had to be performed in order for the two to become the two sources of light that would illuminate the world. 



Tecuciztecatl offered lavish items—“His fir branches \[were] quetzal feathers, and his grass balls \[were] of gold; his maguey spines \[were] of green stone; the reddened, bloodied spines \[were] of coral, and his incense was very good incense”. 



Nanahuatzin on the other hand did not have such costly items to give—“his fir branches were made only of…green reeds bound in threes,... and his grass balls \[were] only aromatic reeds, and his maguey spines were these same maguey spines, and the blood with which they were covered \[was] his own blood, and \[for] his incense, he used only the scabs from his sores, \[which] he lifted up.“



After four days of penance the two gods were tasked with jumping into a bonfire.Tecuciztecatl was called upon to jump first but failed to do so four times as he was scared of the heat. As a result, Nanahuatzin was called and he jumped in immediately, and Tecuciztecatl followed suit. 



**Why do the Sun and the Moon differ?**



Nanahuatzin jumped into the bonfire first so he became the Sun. Since Tecuciztecatl  jumped in second, he became the Moon. They both shone equally bright at first, bothering the gods, who proceeded to rectify this conundrum by throwing a rabbit at the Moon’s face in order to dim it. This is also the reason for the indigenous people describing the full Moon with a rabbit on it. 



**How was the Sun and Moon’s motion explained?**



Both celestial bodies began motionless and right next to each other as shown in the image. The gods decided that sacrifice was needed for the two celestial bodies to move, so the wind Ecatl slew all the gods. One god, Xolotl, tried to escape by transforming into a double maize stalk (xolotl), a double maguey plant (mexolotl), and finally an axolotl, but was finally killed. Even after this sacrifice the Sun and Moon would not move, and so Ecatl blew with all his might eventually setting the two in motion. 



**What did the Spanish think of these fables?**



As Sahagun states in the prologue of book seven, he considered  the Mesoamerican deities to be a form of paganism and he ridiculed their worship including that of the Sun and the Moon. This is seen in the constant refusal to use the same pronouns for the two objects that the Nahuatl text uses. This is also an extension of the fact that Sahagun, throughout the Florentine Codex, uses Euhemerus theory to dismiss the native gods.
