**The Moon**



The Moon, during the start of its waxing phase, was said to look “like a small bow made of thin wire”. During the full moon it rose with the sunset and. It became a full moon after fifteen days, at which point it was described as bright and white and having a white rabbit stretched across its face as shown in the image. 



During the waning phase the moon got smaller and was said to be “dying” and “falleth into a deep sleep”. Towards the new moon it rose with the dawn and it was said that “the moon hath died”.



Once again we see the personification of a celestial body, with the constant repetition of “he” to describe the Moon in the Nahuatl text. It is given human characteristics of sleeping and dying and is further personified in the image with a face on the crescent Moon.   Similar to the Sun, the images of the Moon follow a European convention in the depiction of faces. Also the same as the case of the Sun is the Spanish version of the text using “it” to describe the Moon and actively rejecting the deification of the celestial body.



**What Did the People Think of the Lunar Eclipse?**



During a lunar eclipse the Moon was said to become “dark and sooty”. It was thought to be an ill omen, with pregnant women believing that they would miscarry or have their children turn into mice. The women placed pieces of obsidian in their mouth so that “their children would not be born with mouth eaten away—lipless; or they would not be born with noses eaten away or broken off; or with twisted mouths or lips; or cross-eyed, squint-eyed, or with shrunken eyes; nor would they be born monstrous or imperfect.”
