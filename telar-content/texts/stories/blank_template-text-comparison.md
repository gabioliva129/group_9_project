---
title: "Text Comparison"
---

The Spanish translation is more explanatory and systematic. It says, “the people from Mexico... used to count the years by means of a special wheel with four signs or figures corresponding to the four directions of the world,” which makes the system sound orderly and almost instructional. The Nahuatl version is more active and ceremonial: “One Rabbit... carried, set on its path, took with it, and bore the burden [of the years].” That is a major difference. The Spanish text explains the structure of the calendar, while the Nahuatl text makes the year sign feel alive and burdened with ritual meaning.

The same pattern appears in the treatment of direction. The Spanish says Flint was dedicated to the north “because they believed that the dead went to the north.” The Nahuatl is stronger and more rooted in inherited tradition: “Thus have the old people spoken... when one died... they made him look toward and seated him looking to that place.” The Spanish translates belief into explanation; the Nahuatl preserves voice, memory, and practice.
