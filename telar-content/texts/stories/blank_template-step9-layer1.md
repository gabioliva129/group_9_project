**Text Comparison**

The Spanish translation presents the famine in a more explanatory and observational tone. It states that “Before Ce Tochtli arrived, which they greatly feared because of famine, everyone would try to gather and hide in his or her home many food staples.” The emphasis is on describing what people did to prepare.

The Nahuatl translation focuses more on the experience of fear and scarcity. It says “when [the year] One Rabbit had not yet set in, first provision was made; our food was hidden away, stored, saved up, and placed in bins.” The repeated phrasing emphasizes the urgency of preparation.

Another difference appears in the discussion of slavery. The Spanish text says “many poor men and women would sell themselves as slaves,” presenting this as a social response to famine. The Nahuatl version describes the situation much more vividly: “At this time one sold oneself. One ate oneself; one swallowed oneself. Or else one sold and delivered into bondage his beloved son.” The Nahuatl language highlights the desperation and moral weight of famine more strongly than the Spanish explanation.
