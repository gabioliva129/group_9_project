**Summary: Rain and Water**

In this chapter, we learn of the god Tlaloc who was said to control rain and water, and therefore he made all which they ate, drank, and were sustained and nourished by. This shows that the indigenous peoples understood that water is the force that sustains all living things, and attached a personal and sentient being behind it. They view these naturally-occurring phenomena as gifts and signs from a higher power. Moreover, Tlaloc is one of the most important Aztec gods, shown by the fact that even as the Catholic Church sought to eradicate indigenous religious traditions, Tláloc was still worshipped well after Spanish colonization and depictions of Tlaloc remained within worship spaces for quite some time [1].

Further proving his importance, it is explained that every year they hold a feast for Tlaloc to honor all that he has given them and thank him for sustaining their lives. Before the festival, everyone must fast for four days, and if someone is found to be breaking that fast or disrespecting priests during that time, they would be ostracized and beaten for their disrespectful behavior by everyone in the village.

Also during this festival, the indigenous peoples would dance with both boiled maize rice (which was a common staple food) as well as full stalks of maize as a way to show appreciation for Tlaloc and how he provides food and life.

Lastly, the chapter discusses rainbows and how they were viewed as a sign from Tlaloc that he is taking away his water and influence from an area. Rainbows were seen as a powerful force that broke up clouds and stopped rain from falling, very different to our current understanding of rainbows and how they appear once the rain has stopped on its own. The image of the rainbow from the Florentine Codex shown on this page depicts a very colorful and detailed painting of a rainbow from a native artist’s perspective. They use a lot of very bright hues which would have been difficult to come across at that time. This shows how much spiritual significance was given to rainbows as strong signs of Tlaloc’s power and influence.
